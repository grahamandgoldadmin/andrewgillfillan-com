Five things I actually believe.

Not a philosophy statement. These are the arguments I make in rooms, the ones that shape what my newsroom publishes and how the people in it are treated.

01

Real voices. Not officials.

The podium is the easiest quote in the building and almost never the truest one. I push coverage toward the people living the consequence, and I have been on that side of a bureaucracy myself, going through the U.S. immigration process first hand. It changes what you notice in a story.

02

Depth is the differentiator.

Commodity news is free and infinite. The only defensible thing a local newsroom makes is reporting nobody else has. I ask for depth in every story, every day. Not in a special series twice a year.

03

Distribution is editorial work.

Publishing is not the finish line. Deciding which platform a story belongs on, how it gets promoted and who it speaks to directly is a newsroom judgment, not a marketing afterthought. Great journalism nobody sees is a failure of the newsroom, not the audience.

04

Public media has to earn trust differently.

Public media cannot assume trust because of its history or its mission. We have to demonstrate fairness, independence and intellectual curiosity in every story, while rebuilding products and business models around how audiences actually consume news today. The funding environment is not going to rescue the old way of working. I built a freelance contribution model after federal cuts because the alternative was shrinking the journalism.

05

Treat people right, and grow the underdogs.

Newsrooms run on morale and trust. I empower the people below me to lead, learn what each of them is genuinely good at, and invest hardest in the ones nobody else was investing in. That is not softness. It is how you get work nobody expected.

I work by the numbers. We must reach audiences where they are, and go beyond the headline in every story we tell.
